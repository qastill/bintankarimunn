# Bintan & Karimun Power Systems — 100 Questions & Answers (Investor View)

Public data to 25 Nov 2026 and RUPTL PT PLN (Persero) 2025–2034 (MEMR Decree 188.K/2025), Annex A4 Riau Islands. Each answer: verdict (🟢 positive / 🔴 caution / 🟡 mixed), key figures, points, recommendation, then the full explanation.

## Structure & players

### 1. Who actually supplies electricity on Bintan and Karimun, and how does that differ from Batam?
*General · Data*

**🟡 MIXED** — Three distinct players: PLN Batam (Batam–Bintan system), PLN Persero (retail on Bintan & Karimun Zone 3), and private/captive suppliers in estates.

**Key figures:** **3** supply holders · **2** PLN entities

- • Bintan is retailed by PLN Persero, but its energy comes from the PLN Batam system (inter-licence-area).
- • Karimun Zone 3 is PLN Persero; Zones 1–2 are private (SDU, KPP).
- ⚠️ Two PLN entities mean inter-licence-area negotiation for any large new connection.
- ✅ SEZs and estates may have their own supply (captive or licence area).

> 🎯 **Recommendation:** First map the licence-area holder at the site: it determines your counterparty, tariff and connection cost.

<details><summary>Full explanation</summary>

There are three layers. (1) Bintan (Tanjungpinang City + Bintan Regency) and Karimun Zone 3 are served by PLN (Persero) through UID Riau & Kepri, UP3 Tanjungpinang. RUPTL 2025–2034 defines PLN's Sumatra licence area as covering "Riau Islands except Batam island". (2) Batam is served by PT PLN Batam, a subsidiary with its own RUPTL and tariffs. (3) Certain estates have their own licence area or captive supply: Karimun Zone 1 (PT Soma Daya Utama) and Zone 2 (PT Karimun Power Plant), Galang Batang SEZ (captive coal plant), and Lagoi/Lobam. Physically, the Bintan grid is linked to Batam at 150 kV and operated as a single Batam–Bintan system.

- RUPTL 2025–2034 Ch. I: Sumatra licence area includes the Riau Islands except Batam Island, served by UID Riau and Kepri
- Karimun has 3 zones: Zone 1 SDU, Zone 2 KPP, Zone 3 PLN (detik, 2019)
- PLN Batam reports "Batam–Bintan" load as one system (~780 MW, Oct 2026)

*Source: RUPTL 2025–2034 Ch. I; detik Finance 2019; PLN Batam/InfoPublik Oct 2026*
</details>

*Karimun · Data*

**🔴 CAUTION** — Fragmentation: three small operators with no reserve pooling, making supply costly and fragile.

**Key figures:** **3** licence-area zones · **75%** Zone 2 (KPP) land share

- ⚠️ Zone 1 SDU, Zone 2 KPP (±75% of the island), Zone 3 PLN.
- ⚠️ PLN cannot serve private licence areas.

- • Your site location determines your supplier.

> 🎯 **Recommendation:** Engage the zone holder early; this could become a direct PPA opportunity.

<details><summary>Full explanation</summary>

Under licence-area regulations and local-government recommendations, Karimun is split into three: Zone 1 (PT Soma Daya Utama), Zone 2 (PT Karimun Power Plant, licensed 2015, ±7,326 ha ≈75% of the island), and Zone 3 (PLN). The aim was to attract FTZ industrial-estate investment. The consequence is fragmentation: three small operators, no economies of scale, and PLN cannot serve another party's licence area. This is one of the root causes of Karimun's reliability problems.

### 8. In one minute, how do you assess the power situation on Bintan and Karimun?
*General · Analysis*

**🟡 MIXED** — Bintan: enough power at system level but fragile (one cable). Karimun: short and fragile until the Sumatra cable arrives.

**Key figures:** **±19%** Batam–Bintan reserve · **4.5 MW** Karimun reserve

- ✅ Batam–Bintan has a ±19% reserve and more gas plants coming.
- ⚠️ Bintan depends on one corridor; 15–16 hr blackout on 12 Oct 2026.
- ⚠️ Karimun: 4.5 MW reserve, smaller than one 7 MW coal unit.
- ⚠️ New data-center load will strain both.

> 🎯 **Recommendation:** For critical loads such as data centers, plan dedicated supply + redundancy; don't rely on the grid alone.

<details><summary>Full explanation</summary>

Bintan has enough power at system level (Batam–Bintan ±930 MW supply vs ±780 MW peak) but is topologically fragile. About two-thirds of Bintan's load comes through a single 150 kV corridor from Batam, and local generation is only 30–35 MW. The 12 Oct 2026 blackout proved it: a fault at one substation in Batam blacked out Bintan for 15–16 hours. Karimun is the opposite: no topology problem, but a shortage of firm capacity. Its ±4.5 MW reserve is smaller than the largest 7 MW unit, so a single trip triggers an outage, until the 150 kV cable from Sumatra (RUPTL: TBK substation 2026, realistically 2027–28) arrives. Industrial and data-center growth adds load on top of systems that are already thin.

- Bintan: est. load ±105 MW (last official 65.1 MW, 2016); local ±30–35 MW
- Karimun: ±40 MW TBK system; reserve ±4.5 MW (Nov 2026)
- 12 Oct 2026 blackout: 15–16 hrs, >411k customers

*Source: PLN Batam; ESDM Kepri Oct 2026; Tribun Batam Nov 2026*
</details>

### 10. Does Karimun meet PLN's own reserve criteria?
*Karimun · Data*

**🔴 CAUTION** — Not met. The RUPTL N-2 criterion requires ±50 MW; only 40.6 MW is available.

**Key figures:** **50.1 MW** N-2 requirement · **9.5 MW** shortfall

- ⚠️ Isolated-system criteria: N-2 and a minimum 35% reserve.
- ⚠️ Shortfall of ±9.5 MW.
- ⚠️ Even N-1 (43.1 MW) is not met.
- ✅ The Sumatra cable will end isolated status.

> 🎯 **Recommendation:** Treat PLN supply on Karimun as unreliable for sensitive loads until ±2028.

<details><summary>Full explanation</summary>

No. RUPTL 2025–2034 (II.2.2) requires isolated systems to be planned deterministically to a maximum N-2 criterion: reserve must exceed the first and second largest units, with a minimum 35% reserve margin. For a 36.1 MW load and a largest unit of 7 MW, N-2 requires dependable capacity ≥ 36.1 + 7 + 7 = 50.1 MW, and a 35% RM requires ≥ 48.7 MW. At 40.6 MW, the shortfall is ±8–10 MW. Even N-1 (≥ 43.1 MW) is not met.

- RUPTL II.2.2: "maximum N-2 criterion … reserve margin … minimum 35%"
- Actual reserve 4.5 MW < largest unit 7 MW
- N-1 gap: 2.5 MW; 35% RM gap: 8.1 MW; N-2 gap: 9.5 MW

*Source: RUPTL 2025–2034 Ch. II.2.2; our calculation*
</details>

### 15. How does PLN define reserve margin, and what level is considered optimal?
*General · Data*

**🟡 MIXED** — Reserve margin = (capacity − load) ÷ load; PLN considers ±35% optimal.

**Key figures:** **35%** optimal RM · **19%** Batam–Bintan

- • Large systems are assessed with LOLP; isolated systems with N-2.
- • International benchmark of 15–40%.
- ⚠️ Solar PV is not counted as reserve.
- ⚠️ Batam–Bintan (±19%) and Karimun (±12%) are below optimal.

> 🎯 **Recommendation:** In due diligence, compute the reserve margin after your load is added.

<details><summary>Full explanation</summary>

RUPTL defines RM as generation reserve relative to peak load (capacity/load − 1). Large systems use a probabilistic method (LOLP). PLN studies put the optimal RM at about 33–38% for Java–Bali, against international benchmarks of 15–40%. Isolated systems use deterministic N-2 with a minimum 35% RM. Intermittent renewables are not counted toward reserve unless they provide firm capacity (capacity credit).

- RUPTL Ch. II: optimal RM ±33–38%
- International benchmark 15–40%
- Intermittent RE not counted toward reserve without capacity credit

*Source: RUPTL 2025–2034 Ch. II.2*
</details>

### 18. Why is a 2×7 MW coal plant a problem for a 36 MW system?
*General · Data*

**🔴 CAUTION** — One unit ≈20% of load; a single trip blacks out part of the island immediately.

**Key figures:** **7 MW** largest unit · **±19%** load share per unit

- ⚠️ Small-system inertia is low; frequency falls fast.
- ⚠️ Small coal plants have low availability.
- ✅ Quick fix: grid-forming BESS.
- • Ideal unit size for a 36 MW system is 3–5 MW modular.

> 🎯 **Recommendation:** A 5–10 MW BESS delivers the biggest reliability gain per rupiah.

<details><summary>Full explanation</summary>

From first principles: the largest unit, 7 MW, is ±19% of peak load. When one unit trips, the system loses almost a fifth of its power at once. A small system has low inertia, so frequency falls fast, under-frequency relays operate, and the result can be a total blackout (as in Oct 2026). Small coal plants burning low-rank coal also have low availability (travelling-grate and boiler problems) and ramp slowly. For a 36 MW system, the ideal unit size is ±3–5 MW modular (gas engine/diesel) plus grid-forming BESS for frequency response.

- UFR blackout Oct 2026
- Unit 2 repeated outages in 2022, 2025, May & Jul 2026

*Source: Our analysis; 2026 outage reports*
</details>
